Grammar, Style & Vocabulary Guide

Refining Spoken English for Thesis Oral Defense Examination

1. Nuance & Professional Question Framing

Spoken English

When asking questions during an oral defense, choosing formal vocabulary enhances tone and precision:

Example 1: Referring to Non-Expert Explanations
Informal / Conversational
"How would you explain this to a regular person?"
Academic & Professional (Recommended)
"How would you explain this in intuitive/layman's terms?"
Example 2: Discussing Apparent Contradictions
Informal / Conversational
"These two results look like they disagree with each other."
Academic & Professional (Recommended)
"These two findings might seem contradictory at first glance."

2. Key Grammar & Terminology Pitfalls

Precision
Common Error Correct Academic Term Context / Explanation
An illiquid market (Grammar) An illiquid market Use "an" before vowel sounds ("illiquid").
Lag-0 synchronisation Lag-0 synchrony In time-series analysis, "synchrony" is the standard term for zero-lag alignment.
Counter-party Counterparty Standard financial spelling is a single unhyphenated word.
Underlying capitals Underlying capital "Capital" as financial asset is uncountable in this context.
Spelling Note: Use standard US spelling (e.g., monetized, behavior, analysis) to stay consistent with financial economics literature.

3. Recommended Phrases for Defense Response

Oral Phrases
Expressing Key Concepts Precisely
  • To describe payout structure: "Event contracts feature a binary, all-or-nothing payout structure ($1 or $0)."
  • To describe order matching: "When no counterparty exists on the order book, new pairs are generated through primary market minting and splitting."
  • To explain time lead vs. synchrony: "The early price signal is attributable to the permissionless listing effect rather than faster intra-day price discovery."
